Which part

Dispatch is two jobs. Only one of them is typing.

Most of what gets sold as dispatch automation tries to automate the decisions. This automates the answering, the asking, the quoting and the booking. It leaves the decisions where they belong.

Watch a dispatcher for an hour and the work splits cleanly in half. One half is the phone: pick up, ask the same nine questions, write the answers down, quote from the rate sheet, and book it. The other half is judgment: which car, which chauffeur, what to do when a flight moves, whether this one goes to an affiliate.

The first half is identical on every call and it is where the hours go. The second half is why you hired the person. Automating the first does not touch the second, and any product that claims to automate both is describing a business that does not have exceptions in it.

Automated · the phone part
Answering, asking, writing it up, quoting, booking. Every call, every line at once, whatever time it is. AI Chauffeur takes pickup, dropoff, date and time, vehicle class, passengers, bags, flight and terminal, callback number and notes. Then it reads the trip back and books it.
Not automated · the decisions
Which car, which chauffeur, and what to do when plans change. Your dispatcher assigns the car and the chauffeur. AI Chauffeur does not dispatch, does not change or cancel trips, does not take payment, and does not send work out to an affiliate.
Where the two meet
The trip sheet. Right after the call, your dispatcher has it, with the recording and the transcript. It carries the whole trip, not a summary, so the person assigning the car never has to call anyone back.

What the seat costs

A dispatcher is expensive. So is a phone nobody answers.

A full-time dispatcher runs roughly $45,000 to $60,000 a year in most US markets once payroll taxes and benefits are counted — the loaded cost of the seat, not the number on the offer letter. That is a general market range for the role, not a quote and not a figure from anybody's books.

The seat is worth it. What it does not do is cover 168 hours a week, and the hours it cannot cover are the ones where the calls are hardest to replace: a Friday at 11 PM, a Sunday morning, the forty minutes your dispatcher is on another line.

AI Chauffeur covers the phone part of that job around the clock. This is not a headcount argument. AI Chauffeur does not replace a dispatcher, and nothing on this site will tell you it does. It takes the repetitive half of the work off the seat, so the person in it spends the shift on the half that needs a person. What that is worth to your operation depends on your call volume and your close rate, and neither of those is a number we are going to invent for you.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

What part of dispatch does AI Chauffeur actually automate?
The phone part, and only that: answering the line, asking the whole set of questions, writing the trip up, quoting from your rates, and booking it. Right after the call, your dispatcher has the trip sheet, the recording and the transcript. Everything after that stays with your dispatcher: which car, which chauffeur, any change or cancellation.
Does it book trips by itself?
Yes. It reads the trip back to the caller and books it. Your passenger is booked. A trip your rates don't cover goes to your dispatcher to quote. It does not dispatch. Your dispatcher assigns the car and the chauffeur. AI Chauffeur does not change or cancel trips, and it does not take payment. With your reservation software, the trip is written straight in.
What does a dispatcher stop doing?
Reading voicemail back to themselves, asking the same nine questions for the eleventh time that shift, and calling people back for a bag count. What they keep is the work that needed them in the first place. Nobody's job disappears here; the repetitive half of it does.
Does this work if we already have someone answering the phone?
Yes, and that is the common case. AI Chauffeur works beside the person, not instead of them. It takes the overflow when two lines ring at once, and the hours nobody is sitting there. You pick: 24/7, only after hours, or only the calls your office misses. When a caller asks for a person, it puts the caller through to your team. We go over it on the setup call.
