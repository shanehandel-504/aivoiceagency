No switch, no rip-out

AVA sits on the phone line, not inside your software.

Nothing is installed. Nothing is replaced. Nothing is migrated. There is no plugin to add, no export to run, no records to move, and no login to hand over. AVA answers the phone in front of your operation and writes up what the caller said.

That is why the software question mostly answers itself. Your dispatch system never meets AVA. Your dispatcher does — the ticket comes in, they read it, and they confirm the trip in the same screen they had open yesterday. What sits in front of it all is the limo answering service itself.

Straight answer

AVA captures. Your dispatcher confirms.

Other people in this business blur this part. Here it is straight. AVA answers the phone, takes the whole trip, and writes up a trip ticket. A human on your side reads that ticket and confirms the trip. That is the product today, start to finish.

Until direct write-back has been configured and verified for your account, AVA does not write into your dispatch software at all — and plenty of operators leave it that way on purpose. It never texts or emails the passenger. It does not assign a driver, move a car, or send work out to an affiliate. It does not confirm anything to anybody. All of that stays with your dispatcher, exactly where it sits now.

Who reads the ticket, and how it gets to them, is set on the setup call. That depends on how your shop runs, not on which software you own. How setup works covers the rest of it.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

Do I have to change dispatch software?
No. Nothing is installed, replaced, or migrated. AVA sits on your phone line, in front of everything else you run. Limo Anywhere, FASTTRAK, Santa Cruz, GroundWidgets, Ground Alliance, iCabbi, a shared inbox — keep it, and keep running it the same way.
Does it put the trip into my system for me?
Not on day one. AVA captures the trip and hands your dispatcher a clean ticket, and your dispatcher enters it and confirms it. On supported platforms, direct write-back can be configured and tested against your account and switched on after it verifies, and the record is then created for you. A human confirms every trip either way. That last part is the design and it does not change.
What if I just use a shared inbox?
That works. Plenty of operators run the phone with an inbox and a notepad. The ticket reads the same either way: pickup, dropoff, time, vehicle class, passenger and bag count, flight number and terminal, and notes. Your dispatcher reads it and confirms the trip.
How does AVA work with Limo Anywhere?
It answers the call, takes the whole trip, and delivers a completed trip ticket to the person on dispatch, who enters and confirms it in Limo Anywhere as usual. Direct write-back is available after setup verification, configured around your own account. The field-by-field answer is on the Limo Anywhere page.
How does AVA work with FASTTRAK?
AVA captures the trip on the phone and hands over a finished ticket; your dispatcher keys it in and confirms it. A partner API integration is scoped during setup, alongside the delivery route, the format, and who is on call for it. The corporate-fleet answer is on the FASTTRAK page.
How does AVA work with Santa Cruz?
There is nothing to install on the Santa Cruz side. AVA writes the trip up from the call and sends the ticket to your dispatcher, who confirms the reservation in Santa Cruz. Santa Cruz sits at custom integration review, so anything beyond ticket delivery gets scoped before it is promised. Which fields the ticket carries is decided on the setup call.
What if we just run a shared inbox?
That is a system, and it is the simplest one to hand off to. The completed trip ticket arrives in the inbox your team already watches, and whoever is on dispatch confirms the trip from there. The address and the subject line are agreed during setup.
How is the handoff set up?
On the setup call. Setup is done for you and custom to your operation. The call covers what you already run, who reads the ticket, and how it reaches that person. Rate rules, service area, vehicle classes, and dispatch process get documented, then the build, then test calls, then live. Typically one to two weeks.
