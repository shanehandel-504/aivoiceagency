No switch, no rip-out

AVA sits on the phone line, not inside your software.

Nothing is installed. Nothing is replaced. Nothing is migrated. There is no plugin to add, no export to run, no records to move, and no login to hand over. AVA answers the phone in front of your operation and writes up what the caller said.

That is why the software question mostly answers itself. Your dispatch system never meets AVA. Your dispatcher does — the ticket comes in, they read it, and they confirm the trip in the same screen they had open yesterday.

Straight answer

AVA captures. Your dispatcher confirms.

AVA does not write into your dispatch software. It does not text or email the passenger. It does not assign a driver, move a car, or send work out to an affiliate. It does not confirm anything to anybody. All of that stays with your dispatcher, exactly where it sits now.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

Do I have to change dispatch software?
No. Nothing is installed, replaced, or migrated. AVA sits on your phone line, in front of everything else you run. Limo Anywhere, FASTTRAK, Santa Cruz, GroundWidgets, Ground Alliance, iCabbi, a shared inbox — keep it, and keep running it the same way.
Does it put the trip into my system for me?
No. AVA captures the trip and hands your dispatcher a clean ticket. Your dispatcher enters it and confirms it. AVA does not write into your dispatch software and has no account in it. That is the honest answer, and it is the design — a human confirms every trip.
What if I just use a shared inbox?
That works. Plenty of operators run the phone with an inbox and a notepad. The ticket reads the same either way: pickup, dropoff, time, vehicle class, passenger and bag count, flight number and terminal, and notes. Your dispatcher reads it and confirms the trip.
How is the handoff set up?
On the intro call. Setup is founder-led and custom to your operation. The call covers what you already run, who reads the ticket, and how it reaches that person. Rate rules, service area, vehicle classes, and dispatch process get documented, then the build, then test calls, then live. Typically one to two weeks.
