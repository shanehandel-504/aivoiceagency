What to say

Talk to it like a customer in a hurry.

You do not need a script. The point is to hear how it handles a real call, so give it a real one — including the messy parts.

01

Give it an airport run

A pickup address, a flight number, and how many people. Ask for a curbside meet, then switch to baggage claim halfway through and see whether it keeps up.

02

Change your mind

Move the time. Add a stop. Swap a sedan for an SUV. Mid-call corrections are where most phone systems fall apart, so this is the part worth testing.

03

Ask for the readback

Before you hang up, ask it to repeat the trip. You should hear every detail you gave — and nothing you did not. What it cannot fill in, it asks about.

After you hang up

The trip comes back as a ticket, not a recording.

The call is only half of it. What your dispatcher needs is the trip written down the same way every time — pickup, dropoff, vehicle, passengers, flight, notes.

A detail the caller never gave is marked as missing rather than guessed. Your dispatcher confirms every trip before a car moves. AVA captures the ride; a human on your side still says yes.
