MKE · General Mitchell

The early bank does not wait for a callback.

Milwaukee's first departures push out of General Mitchell while the city is still dark. A passenger who needs a 4:15 AM pickup is calling the night before — at nine, at eleven, at one in the morning. If the line rings out, the run goes to the next name on the list.

AVA answers on the first ring, at any hour. It takes the terminal, the airline, the flight number, the passenger and bag count, and the pickup window your dispatcher needs to build the run sheet.

Flights move. When a caller says the inbound slipped an hour, AVA takes the new time and the new flight number and puts both on the ticket. Your dispatcher confirms it and moves the chauffeur. The full mechanic is on airport transfer booking.

Downtown · Third Ward

The car is supposed to be already there.

Corporate guests do not call early. They call from a hotel lobby or a Third Ward table and expect a car in the valet circle in a few minutes. A ring that goes to voicemail is a ride that never happened — and a guest who tells the front desk you did not pick up.

Valet circles

Downtown pickups are tight. AVA takes the property name, the door the guest will use, and the name on the reservation, so the chauffeur is not circling the block on the phone.

Convention traffic

When a downtown show fills the hotels, the same forty minutes bring a run of calls. AVA answers all of them at once. Nobody waits on hold. Nobody hears a busy signal.

Corporate accounts

Repeat riders say a company name and expect you to know the rest. Account rules and standing preferences are configured during setup, so the ticket comes back the way that account rides.

Summer · the lakefront

Festival nights hit every phone at once.

Milwaukee's summer runs on the lakefront. The festival grounds fill for weeks at a stretch, and the pattern never changes: the calls do not spread out, they land together. Gates open, a set ends, weather turns — and every operator's line rings in the same few minutes.

One dispatcher can hold one call. AVA holds all of them. Every caller gets a live voice, gives a pickup corner and a party size, and comes back as a ticket your dispatcher can sort by time. The same thing happens at one in the morning, which is what after-hours limo dispatch is about.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

Do you only work with Milwaukee operators?
No. AI Chauffeur is built in Wisconsin and runs for chauffeured transportation operators nationwide. Milwaukee is home, so the local detail comes easy — but the service area is set up around your operation, wherever you run.
Does it know MKE and the local hotels?
Your service area, airports, landmarks and hotel list are configured during setup. That includes General Mitchell, the downtown properties you run to most, and the way your dispatcher writes a pickup point. AVA works from your list, not a generic map.
What about festival and event nights?
That is the pattern AVA is built for. Calls do not arrive one at a time on a lakefront Saturday. AVA answers every inbound call at once, 24/7, and never puts a caller on hold or returns a busy signal. Each call comes back as its own trip ticket with the pickup point, party size and time.
Who confirms the trip?
Your dispatcher. AVA captures the call and builds the trip ticket — pickup, dropoff, time, vehicle class, passenger and bag count, flight details and notes. A person on your side confirms it in the software you already run, whether that is Limo Anywhere, FASTTRAK, Santa Cruz or a shared inbox.
