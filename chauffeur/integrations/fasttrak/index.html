Integration · FASTTRAK

AVA works beside FASTTRAK.

Yes — AVA works beside FASTTRAK. It answers every line at once, captures the whole trip, and hands a complete ticket to the dispatcher, who confirms it in FASTTRAK. A partner API integration is scoped during setup and built around your account, not bolted on afterwards.

Last updated 7 August 2026

Where this one stands

Partner API integration, scoped during setup.

That is the honest status and it is the same one printed in the integration status table. Scoped means a real conversation about what your FASTTRAK account supports, what your fleet needs, and what the connection would have to do — held before anyone commits to building it, not after.

What is not in question is the part in front of it. AVA answers your dispatch line from day one and delivers a completed trip ticket to whoever is on dispatch, in the inbox or channel your team already watches. That is included in every setup and depends on nothing at the FASTTRAK end.

Nobody's line waits on connection work. That is why the ticket comes first and the integration is scoped underneath it.

The operation this fits

More cars means more calls that are not simple.

A two-car operator gets one kind of call. A fleet running corporate accounts gets a different one, and it is rarely a passenger booking their own ride. It is an assistant three time zones away, a travel desk with a cost centre to quote against, or a road-show coordinator with six stops and a flight at the end of it.

Those calls are the ones that cost the most to fumble and the ones a rushed intake fumbles first — the booker's name gets taken and the passenger's does not, the account never makes it onto the ticket, and dispatch spends the afternoon calling back to ask.

Corporate accounts

AVA takes the account or company name as spoken and puts it at the top of the ticket, so dispatch can match it to the corporate profile you already keep instead of guessing from a phone number.

Bookers who never ride

Caller and passenger are two different people on most corporate calls. AVA takes both, and takes a callback number for the person who actually needs to be reached when a flight moves.

Multi-stop days

A road show is one booking with a list in it. The stops come down in order, with the timing the caller gave for each, rather than collapsing into a pickup and a dropoff.

Farm-in and farm-out

AVA does not make that call and does not send work anywhere. It puts the complete trip in front of your team early enough that they can make the decision while there is still time to make it.

Every line at once

Three callers at 4 PM on a Thursday get three intakes at 4 PM on a Thursday. No hold queue, no call-back queue, and nothing deciding which caller mattered more.

Your rate rules

Quote ranges come from the rate rules configured for your account during setup. Until those are configured, AVA captures the trip and leaves the number to your team rather than inventing one.

Corporate intake

What AVA asks for, and what it does with the answer.

The corporate call has more moving parts than a night out, and this is the whole list. Anything AVA is not certain of gets read back before the call ends.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

We run a bigger fleet with account managers. Does AVA fit that?
It fits the intake layer of it. AVA answers the lines, takes corporate calls properly — account, booker, passenger, stops, flight, callback — and hands a finished ticket to whoever is on dispatch. Account management, pricing decisions and the relationship stay with the people who own them, and none of that is something AVA touches.
What actually gets scoped during the FASTTRAK setup call?
What your account supports, what your fleet needs, and what a connection would have to do to be worth switching on. Alongside that: your service types, vehicle classes, rate rules, required fields and the approval workflow your dispatchers already use. Twenty minutes, and it ends with a plan rather than a promise.
Does AVA farm work in or out on its own?
No. It does not send work anywhere, accept work from anywhere, or decide that a trip is outside your area. When the caller says something that bears on that, the ticket says it plainly and early — while your team still has time to place the trip rather than after the window has closed.
How does AVA handle a corporate booker who is not the passenger?
As two people, because they are two people. AVA takes the booker's name and callback number, then the passenger's name separately, and puts both on the ticket. That is the single most common way a rushed corporate intake goes wrong, and it is why the question gets asked on the call rather than guessed at afterwards.
