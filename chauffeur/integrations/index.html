The connection ladder

Delivery on day one. Integration after that.

No operator waits on an integration to start capturing calls. The ticket has somewhere to land from the first live call, and the connection is built underneath it while the phone is already being answered.

Rung 1 · Day one
Shared inbox delivery. The completed trip ticket arrives in the inbox or channel your team already watches, addressed and subject-lined the way you agree at setup. Nothing is connected to your dispatch software yet, and nothing needs to be for a trip to get captured and confirmed.
Rung 2 · During setup
The connection is configured and tested. Service types, vehicle classes, rate rules, required fields, statuses and approval workflow get matched to your account. This is the work the setup call scopes, and it is done against your operation rather than against a generic template.
Rung 3 · After verification
Direct write-back is activated — where the platform supports it and your account has passed setup verification. Your dispatcher still confirms every trip inside the approval workflow you already run. Write-back changes where the record is created, not who says yes to it.
Any rung
You can stop at rung one and stay there. Plenty of operators run the whole business off a shared inbox and a dispatcher who reads it, and the product works exactly the same way for them.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

Does AVA connect to my dispatch software?
It can, on supported platforms, once the connection has been configured and tested against your account. Until then — and permanently, if you prefer it that way — AVA delivers a completed trip ticket to the person on dispatch, who enters and confirms the trip in the system you already run. Both are real setups, and the table above says which platforms are at which stage.
What happens on day one, before any integration exists?
The phone gets answered. AVA takes the whole trip and the ticket arrives in the inbox or channel your team already watches, so a call captured on your first live day is a trip your dispatcher can work that same hour. Nothing waits on connection work, which is the point of building the ladder in that order.
My platform is not on the list. Now what?
Shared inbox delivery covers you from day one regardless of what you run, because it depends on nothing at your end. Bring the platform to the setup call and it gets a custom integration review like the rest — that means someone looks at what your account actually supports before anybody promises you anything.
Who confirms the trip once write-back is switched on?
Your dispatcher, exactly as before. Write-back changes where the record gets created; it does not change who approves it. The approval workflow your operation already uses is one of the things configured and tested during setup, and a trip does not skip it because the record arrived automatically.
