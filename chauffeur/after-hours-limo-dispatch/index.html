After-hours dispatch · nights, weekends, surges

After-hours dispatch, answered.

The hours nobody is at the desk are the hours the phone still rings.

The 11 PM window

The show ends and every phone comes out at once.

Lights up, doors open, the sidewalk fills. Some of those people already have a car waiting. The rest start dialing right there on the curb.

Those calls do not arrive politely spaced out. They arrive in a clump — two, then four, then another one while you are still writing down the first. Your desk is dark. The line rings twice and rolls to voicemail, and nobody standing on a cold curb at 11 PM leaves a message.

AVA takes all of them at the same time. No hold, no busy signal, no queue. The fifth caller hears the same greeting as the first. Where you are, where you are going, how many, how much luggage.

By the time the crowd thins out, that run is sitting on the board as a trip ticket with every field filled in.

Saturday, wedding season

One wedding is never one phone call.

A wedding books like a group move and then keeps changing. The party grows. A second pickup gets added on Thursday. Somebody's parents fly in a day early and need a car from the airport.

And the real calls come at the worst hour. Nine at night, mid-reception, the coordinator steps outside because six people now need a ride back to a different hotel than the one on the run sheet. That call does not wait until Monday. If it hits voicemail, they find another car.

The group move

Shuttle runs, staggered departures, two vehicle classes on the same job. AVA takes each leg as its own trip ticket so nothing rides in a note field.

The added pickup

A second address gets tacked on three days out. Time, address, headcount, bags — captured the same as any other run, at whatever hour the call comes.

The mid-reception call

A coordinator with a phone in one hand and a room to run. They get answered on the first ring, they say what changed, and they get back inside.

Every one of those calls ends the same way. A finished ticket sits on the board, ready for a dispatcher on Monday morning. Or in ten minutes, if that is how you run it.

Before the desk opens

The earliest flights are dialing while the office is still dark.

Airport work does not start when your office does. A 7:50 AM departure means a pickup before six, which means the call comes in before five. Business travelers book early and they book fast, because they are already thinking about the security line.

Nobody rebuilt anything from a voicemail. The desk opened to work that was already done, and the early runs were captured hours before anyone unlocked the door.

Overflow and affiliate work

The night every car is already out.

Some Saturdays the whole fleet is committed by eight o'clock. The phone does not know that. It keeps ringing.

And it is not only retail callers. Affiliate work arrives after hours too. Another operator's dispatcher is trying to farm out a run at nine at night. A client moved a pickup, or they are short a car and you are the next name on their list.

If that call dies at voicemail, it is gone twice over. You lose the run, and the operator who called stops calling you first.

AVA answers it. Pickup, dropoff, time, vehicle class, passenger and bag count, who is calling and from what company. The run gets written down whether or not you have a car free.

That is the point. In the morning your dispatcher looks at the board and decides what to do with it. Run it, move a car, deadhead someone over, or farm it out to an affiliate. A human makes that call, not AVA. AVA just makes sure there is something to decide about, because the run was captured instead of lost.

The plain mechanic

An unanswered ring is a fare dialing the next name.

We are not going to put a percentage on this page. We do not have one, and a number we made up would be worth less than nothing to you.

The mechanic is simple, and you already know it. Somebody needs a car. They call. If nobody picks up, they do not sit and wait — they go back to the map and call the next name on it. The ride does not disappear. It moves.

That is the whole cost of after hours. Not a statistic. A transfer.

Answering is what stops the transfer. AVA picks up at 2 AM the same as it does at 2 PM. It takes the entire trip and puts it on the board. The run stays with you, and your dispatcher decides what happens to it next.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

Does it really run at 3 AM?
Yes. AVA answers at 3 AM the same way it answers at 3 PM. There is no hold music, no queue, and no voicemail box. Every inbound call gets picked up at once, and the trip comes back to your dispatcher as a structured trip ticket.
What happens on a holiday weekend?
Nothing changes. New Year's Eve, prom weekend, a snowstorm, the Sunday of a long weekend — AVA takes every call that comes in. That includes the ones that land in the same second. Your desk opens to a stack of trip tickets instead of a full voicemail box.
Can I have it answer only after hours?
Yes. AVA can take the line on a schedule — nights, weekends, holidays, lunch, whatever hours you pick — and hand it back when your desk opens. Or it can take all of it, around the clock. You choose the hours, and you can change them later.
Do I still get to confirm the trip?
Yes. AVA captures, a human confirms. The ticket carries pickup, dropoff, time, vehicle class, passenger and bag count, flight details and notes. AVA does not write into your dispatch software. Your dispatcher confirms the trip in whatever you already run — Limo Anywhere, FASTTRAK, Santa Cruz, or a shared inbox.
