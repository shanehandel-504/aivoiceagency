Madison · Wisconsin-built

Madison limo answering — built in Wisconsin.

Built for Madison limousine, black car, and chauffeured transportation operators. Early departures out of Dane County Regional, corporate riders out of Verona, a downtown of closed streets, and Saturdays that fill the whole city. AVA answers the line while you are driving, dispatching, or asleep — and hands your dispatcher a clean trip ticket.

MSN · Dane County Regional

The early bank calls the night before.

Madison's first departures push out of Dane County Regional before the city is awake. Nobody books a 4:30 AM pickup at 4:30 AM. They call at nine at night, at eleven, at one in the morning — and your office is dark for every one of those calls.

AVA picks up on the first ring, at any hour. It takes the airline, the flight number, the terminal door, the passenger and bag count, and the pickup window your dispatcher needs to build the run sheet.

More on how an airport transfer gets captured, flight and all.

Corporate arrivals run the other direction. A rider lands, walks out, and expects a car at the curb. AVA takes the inbound flight and the arrival time the caller gives, so the meet is on the ticket long before the plane is down. AVA does not watch live flight data today, so a delay nobody calls about is not something it can know. When a caller does report a slip, the new time goes on the same ticket. Your dispatcher confirms it and moves the chauffeur.

State Street · the square · UW

A pickup point downtown is a corner, not an address.

The isthmus is tight. State Street is closed to cars for most of its length. The Capitol square is a ring of one-ways. A campus building has three doors and the rider is standing at the one you did not guess.

So AVA asks what your dispatcher would ask. Which side of the square. Which door. Who is standing there, and what they are wearing if the caller offers it. The corner goes on the ticket, so the chauffeur is not circling the block on the phone.

The square

Callers give a landmark, not a street number. AVA takes the side of the square, the cross street and the name on the reservation, so the pickup is a place a car can actually pull to.

Campus venues

The unions, the arena, a hall up on Bascom — your venue list is configured during setup. AVA works from your list and the doors your chauffeurs actually use, not a generic map pin.

Closed streets

Downtown shuts roads for a Saturday market, a race, a parade. When the usual corner is behind a barricade, the caller's real corner lands in the notes instead of in a callback at midnight.

Camp Randall · game day

A Badger Saturday is one long surge.

Camp Randall sits inside the city, not out past a highway exit. On a home Saturday the neighborhood fills in the morning and lets go all at once in the afternoon. Every operator in Madison feels the same shape: the calls do not spread out, they land together.

One dispatcher can hold one call. AVA holds all of them. Every caller hears a live voice, gives a corner and a party size, and comes back as a ticket your dispatcher can lay out by time. The same shape shows up every night the office is dark, which is after-hours limo dispatch.

Tailgate morning
Group pickups land inside the same half hour. AVA answers all of them at once. No queue to stand in, no second ring.
The start time moves
A shifted kickoff moves every pickup on the board. AVA takes each new time on the call, so your dispatcher opens a ticket that is already current.
The crowd lets out
The neighborhood leaves in one wave and the phone shows it. Nobody hears a busy signal. Each caller gives a corner and a head count, on separate tickets.
Commencement weekend
Families book dinner runs and Sunday departures in the same phone call. AVA takes both legs and neither one waits for the office to open.
Verona · corporate ground

Corporate work out of Verona runs on a calendar.

Verona sits southwest of the city, and a large corporate campus sits with it. That kind of campus brings ground work with it. Visitors in from out of state. Riders moving between a meeting and the airport. Mornings that need two vehicles at the same door.

That work is planned, not spontaneous. It is booked days ahead, often by an assistant in another time zone who has a list to get through and will not call you twice.

AVA answers that call the first time. It takes the company name, the rider, the vehicle class, the pickup window and the return leg, then hands your dispatcher a ticket to confirm. Account rules and standing preferences are configured during setup, so a repeat rider does not spell out the same instructions every trip.

Saturdays · group moves

A wedding is not one trip. It is a run sheet.

Madison books weddings all summer. A ceremony downtown, a reception out by a lake, a hotel block that empties in shifts. The call that starts it is one call. The job is a run sheet: several pickups, a shuttle loop, and a last return long after the office went dark.

Those calls come on weeknights and on the Saturday itself, when your dispatcher is already out on a run. AVA answers every one. It takes each pickup point, each time, the head count and the vehicle class, and the whole move comes back as tickets your dispatcher can put in order.

Plans move on the day. A ceremony runs long, a shuttle needs one more loop, a parent calls to add an address. AVA takes the change on the call and it lands on the ticket. A person on your side confirms every leg before a car turns a wheel.

FAQ

Questions operators actually ask.

Do you only work with Madison operators?
No. AI Chauffeur is built in Wisconsin and runs for chauffeured transportation operators nationwide. Madison is an hour down the road, so the local detail comes easy — but the service area is set up around your operation, wherever you run.
Does it know MSN and the campus venues?
Your service area, airports, venues and hotel list are configured during setup. That includes Dane County Regional, the Capitol square, the campus buildings you run to most, and the way your dispatcher writes a pickup point. AVA works from your list, not a generic map.
What about game days and commencement?
That is the pattern AVA is built for. Calls do not arrive one at a time when a stadium empties or a campus fills with families. AVA answers every inbound call at once, 24/7, and never puts a caller on hold or returns a busy signal. Each call comes back as its own trip ticket with the pickup point, party size and time.
Who confirms the trip?
Your dispatcher. AVA captures the call and builds the trip ticket — pickup, dropoff, time, vehicle class, passenger and bag count, flight details and notes. A person on your side confirms it in the software you already run, whether that is Limo Anywhere, FASTTRAK, Santa Cruz or a shared inbox.
